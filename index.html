02

Clock Tower

The Kowloon Edge

The clock still faces the harbour. The tower rose in 1915 beside the new Kowloon terminus, but its clocks and bell only began keeping time in 1921.

For sixty years, trains left from this shore for the journey north. In 1978 the station was demolished and the tower alone was kept, still standing where those journeys began.

04

Junk with red sails

Red Sails

Before the 1950s, wooden sailing junks were Hong Kong's main fishing boats. The few that sail the harbour now carry visitors, keeping the shape of that memory on the water.

“I can’t change the direction of the wind, but I can adjust my sails to always reach my destination.”

05

Two IFC

City of Light

Two IFC rises 412 metres over Central. Finished in 2003, it was the city's tallest tower for years, and it is still the one on every postcard of the harbour.

06

Fireworks

Afterglow

Fireworks open above the harbour, then soften into smoke and drift slowly over the water.

The light fades, as light does. The water stays. In the morning the ferries will cross again, and the clock will still be facing the shore. The crossing ends; the harbour remains.
