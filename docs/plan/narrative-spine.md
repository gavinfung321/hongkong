# Narrative Spine and Scene 02 Prototype

**Status:** planned 2026-10-04. The current short copy remains the live
baseline until the scene 02 prototype is approved.

## Direction

Keep the site primarily a cinematic 3D harbour crossing. Add a restrained
editorial layer so the journey carries memory, history and emotion instead of
reading only as a sequence of attractive views.

Target balance:

- about 70% continuous 3D experience;
- about 30% text and selective illustration;
- no imitation of Kage's exact layout, artwork or weather effects.

## Narrative arc

| Scene | Story role | Central idea |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Departure | Dusk gathers and the crossing begins |
| 02 | Memory | The Clock Tower remembers the former railway shore |
| 03 | Ritual | The ferry makes an ordinary journey shared across generations |
| 04 | Symbol | Red sails carry Hong Kong's remembered maritime image |
| 05 | Arrival | Central rises as the modern city reached across the water |
| 06 | Afterimage | Fireworks fade while the harbour remains |

## Content format

Each chapter may eventually contain 45–75 words, divided into two short beats:

1. **Observation:** a concrete detail in the present scene.
2. **Meaning or movement:** a historical, human or emotional idea that carries
   the viewer toward the next chapter.

Keep the existing kicker and title. Never show a large paragraph all at once.
The voice remains quiet, specific and close to the water, not promotional or
academic.

All historical claims must be verified with primary or authoritative sources.
Do not invent personal memories or imply that a modern tourist junk is an
unchanged historic working vessel.

- Give every chapter a distinct purpose from the table above.

**01 Departure** (56 words)

> Night gathers on the water. Across the harbour, windows kindle beneath the
> Peak, and their light reaches us in long, broken lines.
>
> People have crossed this water by ferry since 1880, a few minutes between
> Kowloon and the island. Tonight we take the crossing slowly, from the old
> railway clock to the lights of Central.

**02 Memory** (58 words)

> The clock still faces the harbour. The tower rose in 1915 beside the new
> Kowloon terminus, but its clocks and bell only began keeping time in 1921.
>
> For sixty years, trains left from this shore for the journey north. In 1978
> the station was demolished and the tower alone was kept, still standing
> where those journeys began.

**03 Ritual** (58 words)

> Low over the waves, the Star Ferry carries its lit decks toward Central.
> Inside, the wooden seat backs flip over, so passengers always face the way
> they are going. *[verify]*
>
> For most aboard, this is not a view but a commute: a few quiet minutes on
> the water, shared each day by strangers, the same crossing their
> grandparents made.

**04 Symbol** (56 words)

> A junk with red sails passes close enough to fill the night, its battened
> canvas catching the city's light, its dark hull slipping through the
> reflections.
>
> Before the 1950s, wooden sailing junks were Hong Kong's main fishing boats.
> The few that sail the harbour now carry visitors, keeping the shape of that
> memory on the water.

**05 Arrival** (31 words; rewritten to be about Two IFC alone, user
request, 2026-10-04; the reclamation draft was dropped)

> Two IFC rises 412 metres over Central. Finished in 2003, it was the city's
> tallest tower for years, and it is still the one on every postcard of the
> harbour. (Verified 2026-10-04; sources under Scene 05.)

**06 Afterimage** (49 words)

> Fireworks open above the harbour, then soften into smoke and drift slowly
> over the water.
>
> The light fades, as light does. The water stays. In the morning the ferries
> will cross again, and the clock will still be facing the shore. The crossing
> ends; the harbour remains.

Sources checked:

- 01, 03: Star Ferry origins in 1880 (`FINAL-NARRATIVE-COPY.md` §10).
