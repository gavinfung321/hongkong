## Voice

The narrator is quiet, observant and close to the water. It notices
specific things without becoming a tour guide.

Use present tense, short concrete sentences, and place names only when
they orient the crossing.

Avoid tourism language, generic neon language, a claim that the harbour
is unchanged, calling the red-sailed vessel an ancient working junk, and
any suggestion that fireworks happen every night. Do not repeat what the
picture already shows.

The site speaks English, with selected Traditional Chinese labels. A full
Chinese version would need its own layouts.

## Chapters

### 01 Harbour at Dusk

**Kicker:** Victoria Harbour  
**Label:** 維港

> Night gathers on the water. Across the harbour, windows kindle beneath the
> Peak, and the night crossing begins.

### 02 The Kowloon Edge

**Kicker:** Clock Tower  
**Label:** 鐘樓

On a phone only the first paragraph shows. The photograph is desktop only.

> The clock still faces the harbour. The tower rose in 1915 beside the new
> Kowloon terminus, but its clocks and bell only began keeping time in 1921.

> For sixty years, trains left from this shore for the journey north. In 1978
> the station was demolished and the tower alone was kept, still standing
> where those journeys began.

**Print:** Kowloon terminus, ca. 1915 · Hong Kong Public Libraries

**Timeline:** 1915 Tower rises · 1921 Clocks begin · 1975 Last train · 1978
Station demolished

### 03 Across the Water

**Kicker:** Star Ferry  
**Label:** 天星小輪

The meaning line is desktop only. On a phone, “Crossing since 1888” sits
under the standfirst. On desktop that line is read by assistive technology,
and the countdown is captioned **Since 1888**.

> Kowloon to the Island, the slow way, every few minutes.

> Not a view but a commute: a few quiet minutes, the same crossing their
> grandparents made.

**Year:** Crossing since 1888

**Board:** Star Ferry 天星小輪 · To 往 · Due 到達 · Tsim Sha Tsui 尖沙咀 ·
Central 中環. The due line counts 3 MIN, 2 MIN, 1 MIN, ARRIVING.

**Ticket:** A Star Ferry ticket: upper deck, Tsim Sha Tsui to Central,
adult weekday fare HK$5.0

The footer says the ferry’s origins are in 1880. This chapter keeps 1888.
Both stay (user choice, 2026-10-06). 1880 is the first crossing, Dorabjee
Naorojee Mithaiwala’s steamboat Morning Star. 1888 is the year the Kowloon
Ferry Company, the direct precursor, was founded and registered.

### 04 Red Sails

**Kicker:** Junk with red sails  
**Label:** 帆船

> Before the 1950s, wooden sailing junks were Hong Kong's main fishing boats.
> The few that sail the harbour now carry visitors, keeping the shape of that
> memory on the water.

**Then:** Before 1945  
**Now:** Dukling, built 1955

> “I can’t change the direction of the wind, but I can adjust my sails to
> always reach my destination.”

### 05 City of Light

**Kicker:** Two IFC  
**Label:** 國金

> Two IFC rises 412 metres over Central. Finished in 2003, it was the city's
> tallest tower for years, and it is still the one on every postcard of the
> harbour.

**Callouts:** desktop “Light the skyline” and “Hover me”. Phone “Tap me”
for both.

### 06 Afterglow

**Kicker:** Fireworks  
**Label:** 煙花

The picture has fireworks. The words do not say they happen every night.

> The light fades, as light does. The water stays. In the morning the ferries
> will cross again, and the clock will still be facing the shore. The
> crossing ends; the harbour remains.
